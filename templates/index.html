Abstract

Diffusion-based talking-face generation has achieved strong visual quality, yet reconstruction-oriented objectives do not explicitly optimize how accurately generated facial motion follows the landmark condition. Several studies introduce auxiliary losses to improve motion accuracy; however, diffusion models produce reliable visual structures mainly at later denoising steps. Applying such supervision to early noisy states may therefore provide inaccurate or misleading gradients and interfere with the original denoising objective. To address this mismatch, we propose Talk-GRPO, a reward-based post-training framework that evaluates the quality of the final generated output rather than imposing additional supervision throughout the denoising process. Given a reference image and driving facial landmarks, Talk-GRPO samples multiple candidate outputs and evaluates them using an explicit geometric reward derived from facial landmarks together with a perceptual preservation term. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) then converts these final-output rewards into relative advantages, enabling direct optimization of landmark fidelity without differentiating through the reward evaluator. We further refine the temporal-attention modules with timestep-adaptive perceptual supervision to improve temporal stability. Experiments on multiple datasets show that Talk-GRPO improves mouth-landmark fidelity and temporal consistency while preserving visual quality. In addition, under accurate full-landmark conditioning, Talk-GRPO outperforms supervised fine-tuning methods in the landmark-conditioned rendering stage while preserving reconstruction quality.

Pipeline Overview

1. Group Generation Given the same reference image, driving landmarks, and audio condition, the generator samples multiple candidate outputs.
2. Final-Output Reward Each candidate is evaluated at the final generated state using landmark-based mouth fidelity and perceptual preservation rewards.
3. Two-Stage Post-Training Stage 1 uses GRPO to improve landmark fidelity, while Stage 2 refines temporal-attention modules to enhance temporal stability.

Ablation Studies

We analyze the contribution of each training stage to landmark fidelity and temporal stability. Stage 1 primarily improves landmark fidelity through GRPO post-training, while Stage 2 mainly enhances temporal consistency through temporal-attention refinement.

For landmark fidelity, most of the MLD improvement is already obtained in Stage 1. GRPO post-training reduces MLD by approximately 15.6% on MEAD and 20.2% on HDTF, while CREMA-D shows a smaller reduction of about 6.1%. In contrast, Stage 1 contributes only minor improvements to temporal consistency, while Stage 2 reduces FLVD by more than 40% on all three datasets. These results show the complementary roles of the two stages: Stage 1 mainly improves landmark fidelity, whereas Stage 2 is responsible for most of the temporal gain.
